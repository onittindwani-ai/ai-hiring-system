# 11. References, offers and onboarding

## Purpose
Check what you were told, make an offer people say yes to, close fairly, and get the new person productive in 90 days.
A hire is not done at "yes". It is done when they deliver the 90 day outcomes.

## Do this: reference checks
1. Ask for references only from finalists, after the work sample, and with their OK. Tell them you will call and what you will ask. Never contact their current employer without permission.
2. Ask for people who saw the work: a manager, a peer, a client. Two or three, not ten.
3. Use the template (`templates/interviews/reference-check.md`). Same questions for every finalist, tied to the scorecard.
4. Questions that tell you something (open, specific):
   - "What did {{name}} own, and how did it go?"
   - "What would you want them to improve at?" (people answer this honestly when it is framed as growth)
   - "How did they handle a mistake or a hard deadline?"
   - "Would you work with them again, and in what kind of role?" (listen to the pause)
   - "Is there anything we should know to set them up for success?"
5. Say what you will do with the answers. Take notes of what was said. Mark each note as fact or opinion.
6. Compare with the scorecard evidence. A reference confirms or questions what you saw. It does not replace it.
7. Treat a weak reference as a question, not a verdict: ask the candidate a fair follow up. Many managers give short, safe or biased answers.
8. Do not do social media digging for protected traits. Do not ask former employers for health, family or pay history. Public information about the work is fine.
9. Background or credential checks (identity, right to work, licences, criminal record where the job truly needs it) follow local law and need written consent. This kit does not run them. Verify locally what is allowed and how (doc 12).

## Do this: offer design
1. Decide the offer package before the final interview, from the budget plan (doc 02) and market pay (`pay_research_hint` in the scorecard). Write down the range, the target and the walk away point.
2. Offer in this order: a call (so they hear the "yes" from a person), then a written offer within 24 hours (use `templates/interviews/offer-checklist.md`).
3. Put in the written offer: title, start date, pay and how it is paid, pay period, hours, work location or remote terms, reporting line, probation or trial terms, benefits and time off, any bonus rules, what happens if it does not work out, the answer deadline, and what you need from them (documents, references, consent for checks).
4. Employee or contractor: say which, and why (doc 14). Do not call a person a contractor to save cost if the work is really employment. Verify locally.
5. Same role, same level, same pay logic for everyone. Do not base the number on what they used to earn. Use the role's range and their evidence against the scorecard.
6. Give a reasonable deadline (3 to 5 working days is common, starting assumption). Be available for questions.
7. Read the offer aloud once. Run `tools/bias_check.py` and `tools/message_check.py` on the cover message. Have the owner approve. A person signs and sends it. Claude never sends an offer.

## Negotiation
- Prepare: know your range and which items can move (start date, hours, title wording, learning budget, signing bonus, review date). Know which cannot (pay band limits, fairness to the team).
- Listen first. Ask what matters most to them: money, flexibility, growth, security.
- Trade, do not just give: "we can move the start date and add a 6 month review, but the base stays."
- Be honest about limits. Do not invent a competing offer or a "deadline from my boss".
- Do not cut the offer because they asked. Asking is normal.
- Fairness check: before you agree to above range pay, ask "would I give this to a person like this in the same role, whoever they are?". Document the reason (evidence of extra skill, not a good negotiator).
- Put the final agreement in writing the same day.

## Closing and handling "no"
- Close with energy and clarity: say why you want them (one true, specific reason from the evidence), what their first month looks like, who they will work with.
- A good close has a next step with a date: "sign by Friday, start on the 1st."
- If they decline: thank them, ask what tipped it (pay, role, timing, another offer), and ask whether they know someone. Record the reason, it is free market research.
- If they ask for more time: set an end date, and tell your backup candidates you are still in touch.
- Keep your second choice warm: honest updates, no promises you cannot keep, no "holding" someone for weeks.
- Never exploding offers ("answer in 2 hours") for regular roles.
- If an accepted offer falls apart (no show, a withdrawal): move to the next candidate on the backup list quickly, and review what you could have done earlier.

## Do this: onboarding 30 60 90
Use `templates/interviews/onboarding-30-60-90.md`. The goal: a clear first week, real work by week 2, and a measured result by day 90.
1. Before day 1: send the logistics (when, where or link, who to ask for, what to bring), sign the paperwork, set up accounts and tools, tell the team who is joining and why they are great (true facts only).
2. Day 1: welcome, meet the manager, a short plan for the week, access to tools, one small useful task.
3. Week 1: a buddy, a map of the business (customers, tools, where files live), 2 or 3 starter tasks, daily 10 minute check in.
4. Day 30: learn the work and the people. Output: can do the basic tasks without help. A review conversation.
5. Day 60: do the work on their own on the main tasks. Output: the first of the 90 day outcomes is in sight. A review conversation.
6. Day 90: deliver the outcomes written in the scorecard (`outcomes_90_days`). A review and a plan for the next 90 days.
Example (made up, bookkeeper at Northlight Home Services):
| When | Goal |
| --- | --- |
| Day 30 | does bank reconciliation for 2 accounts, with review |
| Day 60 | runs month end close alone, owner reviews the result |
| Day 90 | close done by working day 5 each month, unclear items listed with questions |

## Offer checklist (what a clear offer includes)
| Item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Job title and one line mission | avoids "that was not the job I was told" |
| Pay, pay period, currency, how it is paid | the number they will judge you by |
| Bonus or commission rules in writing | variable pay is the top cause of disputes |
| Hours, days, time zone, work location | sets expectations for remote and hybrid |
| Start date and first day plan | lowers the chance of a no show |
| Employee or contractor, and the contract to sign | legal clarity (verify locally) |
| Benefits, leave and holidays you really offer | never list a benefit you do not have |
| Trial period terms, if local law allows | no surprise later |
| Answer deadline | respects both sides |
| Who to call with questions | shows you are reachable |

## Early warning signs in the first 90 days
- The new person is not clear what "good" looks like: your outcomes were vague. Fix the plan, not the person.
- Questions stop coming: they may be stuck or afraid to ask. Ask them directly in the check in.
- Output is fine but they are isolated: add a buddy and one introduction a week.
- Output is behind at day 30: ask what is blocking (access, tools, unclear priorities) before judging skill.
- A pattern of small misses after clear feedback: write it down, give one more chance with a specific plan and a date, then decide.
Treat early signals as information about your process first. Hiring mistakes are partly onboarding mistakes.

## Backup plan for a failed hire
1. Keep the top 2 finalists in a warm, honest list for 90 days after an offer (with their OK).
2. Keep the scorecard and the work sample ready so a new search starts in days, not weeks.
3. If you part ways early, review three things: what the evidence said, what you ignored, what the onboarding missed. Update the scorecard.

## Probation or trial period
- Many places use a probation (trial) period. Local law decides if it is allowed, how long, and what notice is needed. Verify locally (doc 12).
- Use it to check fit, not to avoid giving feedback. Review at 30, 60 and 90 days against the written outcomes.
- If it is not working: say it early, with examples, and a plan to improve. Surprises are unfair.
- If you part ways, follow local law for notice, pay and paperwork. Ask a local adviser before ending any employment.
- Write down the decision and the reason at the end of probation, whether you keep them or not.

## Common mistakes
- Reference check as a formality, with soft questions.
- Calling the current employer without consent.
- Offering a number based on their past pay.
- A vague offer ("we will figure out the title later").
- Slow offers. Good people vanish in a week.
- Squeezing a person in negotiation, then wondering why they leave in 6 months.
- No plan for day 1, so the new person sits waiting for access.
- No 30 60 90 reviews, then a surprise at month 4.
- Treating onboarding as "HR forms" only.

## Numbers
- Offer deadline: 3 to 5 working days (starting assumption).
- Written offer within 24 hours of the call (starting assumption).
- Reviews at day 30, 60, 90 (common practice, adjust to your role).
- Probation length: set by local law and contract. Do not copy another country's number.
- Acceptance rate to plan: use your own data. As a starting assumption use 80 percent for a normal role (the funnel tool's number), and overhire offers if you need N hires at once (`tools/funnel_calculator.py`).
- Cost of a bad hire: use your own estimate. Formula: months to notice x fully loaded monthly cost + hiring cost + rehire cost (`tools/budget_planner.py` prints the parts).

## Ask the owner
1. What is the pay range, the target and the walk away point? Default: range from the scorecard research, target at the middle, walk away at the top.
2. Employee or contractor, and what trial period? Default: employee for ongoing work, trial period only where local law allows (verify).
3. Which references do you want, and who asks them? Default: two, asked by the hiring manager, with the candidate's OK.
4. Who is the buddy and who does the 30 60 90 reviews? Default: the manager does the reviews, a teammate is the buddy.
5. What are the 3 outcomes at day 90? Default: copy `outcomes_90_days` from the scorecard.
