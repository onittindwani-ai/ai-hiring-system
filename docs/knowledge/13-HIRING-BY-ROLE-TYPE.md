# 13. Hiring by role type

## Purpose
The funnel is the same for every job, but what you test, where you look, and what goes wrong is different.
Use this as a quick "what is special about this role" guide, then build the scorecard (doc 05).

## How to use this
1. Find the role type below. Read "what to test", "best evidence" and "watch out".
2. Copy the closest template from `templates/scorecards/` and edit it to your business.
3. Every table row is a starting point. Replace with your own data, and keep every criterion job related (doc 12).

## At a glance
| Role type | Best evidence | Typical work sample | Special risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales | past results with numbers, a live role play | write outreach and handle an objection | smooth talkers; numbers that cannot be checked |
| Customer support | real tickets, tone, patience | answer 4 sample tickets | burnout, tone in writing, shift fit |
| Operations | checklists, systems, follow through | plan a small schedule or fix a messy process | "does everything" without finishing |
| Finance, bookkeeping | accuracy, close process, tools | reconcile a small fake file | money risk, access and controls |
| Marketing, content | portfolio with results | a short plan or a draft piece | vanity metrics, copied work |
| Creative, video | portfolio and a paid test | edit or design a small sample | taste mismatch, unlicensed work in the portfolio |
| Engineering | code they wrote, a real task | small take home or pair session | whiteboard tricks, resume stuffing |
| Assistants | reliability, judgment, discretion | triage a messy inbox and calendar | trust and confidentiality |
| Managers | how their people did, a case | a 30 60 90 plan for your team | great doers who dislike managing |
| Contractors | finished past work, references | a paid, scoped trial | scope creep, classification (doc 14) |
| First hire | range of skills, ownership | a real first month plan | wrong profile, no support |
| Key early employee | judgment, ownership, values you can name | a paid project and a long conversation | high cost of a miss |

## Sales
- What to test: finding or getting leads, talking to people, following up, handling "no", and writing clearly. Pipeline hygiene and honesty.
- Best evidence: past quotas and results, with a way to check (a reference, a pay record is not needed). Ask "what was the number and how did you get it?".
- Work sample: write a first message to 3 fictional leads, reply to an objection, and say what you would ask on a call. Role play a 10 minute discovery call.
- Pay: often base plus variable. Write the variable rules clearly and fairly.
- Watch out: a great pitch is not great selling. Check listening and follow up. Do not hire only for confidence.
- Honest note: a sales development rep (SDR) job is the part of sales that books meetings. Many SDR tasks can be done partly by tools (doc 04). Decide the split first.

## Customer support
- What to test: clear writing, empathy, patience, problem solving, reading carefully, product learning speed.
- Best evidence: sample replies, how they handle an angry ticket, a short test on a policy.
- Work sample: 4 tickets (one angry, one unclear, one needing a refund decision, one needing escalation).
- Fit details: shifts, time zone, language needs. Say them in the post. Do not score accent. Test language only to the level the job needs.
- Watch out: burnout. Ask about rotation, breaks and escalation in the interview and in the job.
- Automation note: many first line questions can be answered by a tool. Hire people for the hard cases (doc 04).

## Operations
- What to test: process thinking, follow through, working across people, small details, and calm under pressure.
- Best evidence: a story where they improved a process, with a before and after.
- Work sample: a messy week of jobs and staff to schedule. Ask what they would do first and what they would fix long term.
- Watch out: a person who says "I do everything" and cannot show a system. Look for checklists and handover.

## Finance and bookkeeping
- What to test: accuracy, close process, reconciliations, tools you use, and judgment about when to ask.
- Best evidence: the tools and steps they actually used, month end details, how they caught an error.
- Work sample: a small fake bank file and ledger to reconcile, plus 3 questions for the owner.
- Money risk: separate duties, limit access at first, use approvals. Never give full payment access on day 1. Verify identity and right to work locally.
- Credentials: ask for licences or certification only if the job or the law truly needs them. A licence is a gate, not a score.
- Watch out: a polished resume with no detail. Ask them to walk through a close step by step.

## Marketing and content
- What to test: strategy, writing or design, results, and honest reporting.
- Best evidence: a portfolio with what they did, the goal and the result. Ask "what part was yours?".
- Work sample: a one page plan for a fictional offer, or a draft piece with a clear brief. Pay if over an hour.
- Watch out: vanity metrics (views with no business result), copied work, and AI generated work presented as hand made. Say whether AI tools are welcome.
- Check ownership: ask whether the portfolio work can be shown and who owns it.

## Creative and video
- What to test: taste, speed, feedback handling, tool skill, and reliability.
- Best evidence: a reel or portfolio plus a short paid test on a clip you provide. Check that the portfolio is theirs and they have the rights to show it.
- Work sample: edit a 60 second clip to a brief. Score structure, pacing, sound, and how they follow notes. Blind where possible.
- Watch out: taste mismatch is real but vague. Write what you want in words and examples in the scorecard so it can be scored.
- Contractor or employee: many creatives are contractors. See doc 14.

## Engineering
- What to test: writing and reading code, debugging, design judgment, communication, learning.
- Best evidence: code they wrote (public or shown), a real small task, and how they explain choices.
- Work sample: a small take home with a time cap or a pair session on a simple real problem. Avoid puzzles that have nothing to do with the job.
- Watch out: keyword stuffing of tools, inflated titles, and "10 years of experience" used as a proxy for skill (and for age). Score what they built.
- Automation note: coding tools change how engineers work. Test whether they can use them well and still understand the code.

## Assistants
- What to test: reliability, judgment, discretion, written clarity, tools, and how they prioritise.
- Work sample: a messy inbox and calendar to triage, with 3 traps (a double booking, a rude email, a confidential item). Score the choices and what they ask.
- Trust: be clear about access and confidentiality from day 1. Start with limited access and widen it.
- Automation note: a lot of scheduling and drafting can be tool supported. Define the human part: judgment and relationships.

## Managers
- What to test: how people they managed grew, how they give feedback, how they make decisions, how they handle conflict.
- Best evidence: references from people they managed (with consent), a case about your team.
- Work sample: a 30 60 90 plan for a described team, or a conversation about a hard feedback case.
- Watch out: a star doer who does not want to manage. Ask what they enjoy about managing, and what they would stop doing.

## Contractors
- What to test: finished work, scope clarity, communication, reliability.
- Process: a paid, small, scoped trial first. Written scope, deliverables, deadline, fee, who owns the work, and how to end the deal.
- Watch out: scope creep, vague hours, and misclassification. If you control how, when and where the work is done like an employee, the law may call them an employee. Verify locally (doc 12 and 14).

## Your first hire
- Different goals: you need range, ownership and the ability to work without a manager. Write the first 90 days as outcomes, not tasks.
- A "jack of all trades" is OK, but be honest about the job. Do not promise growth you cannot give.
- Ask: "what will you do when nobody tells you what to do?".
- Make sure you have time to onboard them. A first hire without support often leaves.
- Consider a contractor or part time trial before a full time hire, if law and the work allow it.
- Check budget and hire or automate again (docs 02 and 04).

## Hiring a key early employee
- This person shapes the company. A miss is costly in money and trust.
- Use more evidence, not more rounds: a paid project (2 to 5 days at most, starting assumption), 2 or 3 references from people who saw their work, and a long honest talk on how you will work together.
- Write the values you want as behaviors (for example "tells me bad news early"), never as "culture fit" or traits.
- Share the real picture: money, risks, and what is not working. Surprises later cost more.
- Consider equity or bonus only with a lawyer and clear written terms. Not a promise made in a chat.
- Decide your top three non negotiables in writing before you meet anyone.

## Common mistakes
- Using one scorecard for every role.
- Testing skills the job never uses.
- Hiring for personality in roles where results can be tested.
- Skipping the work sample for roles that make visible things.
- Over trusting a portfolio or a number you cannot check.
- Hiring a contractor and managing them like an employee.
- Hiring a first employee before you know what you want them to do.
- Giving a new finance person broad access on day 1.

## Numbers
- Work sample length: 30 to 90 minutes for most roles. Key early hire projects up to a few days, paid (starting assumptions, replace with your own data).
- Funnel ratios differ by role: use `tools/funnel_calculator.py` for the math, then replace with your real conversion rates after the first batch.
- Pay: no invented numbers. Use `pay_research_hint` in each scorecard to find the market range, with a source and a date.

## Ask the owner
1. Which role type is this closest to? Default: the closest template in `templates/scorecards/`.
2. Is this role employee, contractor or part time? Default: the one that matches how much control you have over the work (verify locally).
3. What should a great person have finished in 90 days? Default: 3 outcomes, you approve.
4. What can a tool already do in this role? Default: Claude lists it from the scorecard `automation_note`.
5. What is the one thing that would make you say no immediately? Default: only a job related reason, written down.
