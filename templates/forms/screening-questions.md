# Screening question bank (30 questions)

Use this to fill the three role slots in `application-form.json` or to run a short first call. Pick questions that match your scorecard's `must` criteria. Three to six questions is plenty. Every applicant for the same role gets the same questions.

## Rules for screening questions

- Job related only. Each question should map to a scorecard criterion or to logistics.
- Lawful. Never ask about age, date of birth, gender, photo, marital status, children or family plans, nationality or origin, religion, health, disability, accent, or where someone grew up. Rules differ by region. Not legal advice: check with an adviser. <!-- bias-check: guidance -->
- Open questions with a real example beat yes or no questions. Yes or no questions are easy to game.
- No automatic knock outs, except a clear legal or schedule gate that a person confirms.
- Answers are scored on evidence (what they did, what happened), not on how polished the writing is, unless writing is the job.
- If a candidate needs another format (spoken, written, more time), allow it.

## Real experience

1. **Tell us about the most relevant piece of work you have done for this kind of job. What was your part, and what happened?**
   - Good answer shows: A real, specific example with their own role and a result. Vague group credit is a weak sign.

2. **What is the hardest problem you have solved in this type of work, and how did you solve it?**
   - Good answer shows: Steps, reasoning and a result, not only the tool they used.

3. **Describe a typical week in your most recent relevant work. What took most of your time?**
   - Good answer shows: Realistic detail. Shows whether the work really matches ours.

4. **Name one thing you did in a past job that you would do differently today, and why.**
   - Good answer shows: Honest reflection and learning, not a polished brag.

5. **What is one result from your work that you can show us or describe with numbers?**
   - Good answer shows: A number or a visible result and how they measured it. A missing number is not a fail: ask in the interview.

## Problem solving

6. **A customer or coworker gives you a request that is unclear. What do you do first?**
   - Good answer shows: Asks questions, restates the need and checks, before building anything.

7. **Two tasks are both urgent and you can only do one today. How do you choose?**
   - Good answer shows: A clear rule (impact, deadline, who is waiting) and telling people about the trade off.

8. **Describe a time something broke or went wrong on your watch. What did you do in the first hour?**
   - Good answer shows: Calm triage, telling people early, a fix and a lesson.

9. **How would you find out why a number or a result in your work looked wrong?**
   - Good answer shows: A step by step check: source, change, comparison. Shows habit of verifying.

## Communication

10. **Explain something you know well to a person who does not know it, in 4 sentences.**
   - Good answer shows: Plain words, order, no jargon. Shows how they will write to customers and teammates.

11. **Write the first two lines of a message to a customer who is upset about a late job.**
   - Good answer shows: Acknowledges the problem, no excuses, a next step.

12. **Tell us about a disagreement with a coworker or customer. How did it end?**
   - Good answer shows: Listens, finds common ground, takes responsibility where fair. Avoids blaming.

13. **How do you keep other people updated when your work is slow or blocked?**
   - Good answer shows: Early, short updates with a plan, not silence.

## Ownership and reliability

14. **Tell us about a time you finished something nobody checked. Why did you do it?**
   - Good answer shows: Internal drive and a real example.

15. **How do you keep track of what you have to do and by when?**
   - Good answer shows: A real system (calendar, list, tracker) rather than 'I remember'.

16. **Describe a deadline you missed or nearly missed. What happened?**
   - Good answer shows: Honesty, what they changed after, and early warning to others.

17. **When do you ask for help, and when do you try alone first?**
   - Good answer shows: A sensible balance with a time limit and a clear question.

## Learning and feedback

18. **What is something you learned in the last six months for your work? How did you learn it?**
   - Good answer shows: Real, specific learning with a method. Shows growth.

19. **Tell us about feedback you received that was hard to hear. What did you change?**
   - Good answer shows: Specific change after feedback, not defensiveness.

20. **How do you get up to speed in a new tool or process quickly?**
   - Good answer shows: Docs, practice, asking questions, small tests. A plan, not a hope.

## People and customers

21. **Describe a time you went beyond what was asked for a customer or coworker. What was the result?**
   - Good answer shows: Real care with a boundary: not breaking rules or promising what they cannot.

22. **What does good service mean to you in this job? Give an example.**
   - Good answer shows: Concrete behaviors (answering fast, honest limits, follow up).

23. **Tell us about a time you had to say no to someone. How did you do it?**
   - Good answer shows: Clear, kind, and offers an alternative.

## Tools and AI

24. **Which tools do you use most in your work, and what is one thing you set up in them yourself?**
   - Good answer shows: Names tools and something they built or configured.

25. **If you use AI tools in your work, how do you check their output before using it?**
   - Good answer shows: Checks facts, keeps private data out, knows its limits. 'I do not use any' is also a fine answer.

26. **What would you never give to an AI tool to handle alone in this job, and why?**
   - Good answer shows: Judgment about risk (money, private data, people decisions).

## Practical questions

27. **Are you legally able to work in {{country}} for this role?**
   - Good answer shows: A yes, no or 'let us talk'. This is a gate for a person to follow up on, not an automatic decision.

28. **Can you work the schedule described in the post? Tell us if a small change would help.**
   - Good answer shows: Fit with the real schedule. Flexible answers are fine.

29. **When could you start, and is there a notice period we should know about?**
   - Good answer shows: Logistics only. Never score this.

30. **What pay are you hoping for, and what would you like to know about our range of {{pay_range}}?**
   - Good answer shows: Optional. Only use to avoid wasting time. Never use a person's past pay to set theirs.

## How to score answers

- Use the 0 to 4 levels from the scorecard. Level 3 means they did it on their own with a clear result.
- Write one short evidence quote for every level above 0.
- Thin answers are not wrong answers. Short writing may come from busy people or people writing in a second language. If the work looks promising, ask a follow up in the interview.
- Check the first and last question you scored again at the end, to catch drift.
- A person makes every decision. A score is a reason to look, not a verdict.
