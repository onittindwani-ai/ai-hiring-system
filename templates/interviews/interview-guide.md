# Interview guide: {{role_title}}

A structured interview: the same questions, in the same order, for every candidate for this role, scored against the same scorecard. This is fairer and gives a better signal than a free chat. `python3 tools/interview_kit.py hiring/<role>/scorecard.json` fills the question part from your scorecard.

Interviewer: {{interviewer_name}}   Candidate: {{candidate_id}}   Date: {{interview_date}}   Length: {{interview_length}} minutes

## Before the interview (5 minutes)

- [ ] Read the scorecard and the candidate's redacted summary. Do not look up their social media.
- [ ] Open the rating sheet (`rating-sheet.md`) next to this guide.
- [ ] Test the room or the video link. Have the pay range ready: {{pay_range}}.
- [ ] Write down the questions you will ask. Do not add personal questions on the day.

## Opening (3 minutes)

Say: "Thank you for your time. Today I will ask about real situations from your work. I will take notes so I can score fairly. At the end you can ask me anything. Is there anything that would make this easier for you?"

Explain the steps after this call and when they will hear from you.

## Core questions (about 30 minutes)

Ask one question per must criterion first, then the nice to have ones if time allows. Use the same follow ups each time.

| # | Criterion (from scorecard) | Question (from scorecard) | Follow ups |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | {{criterion_1}} | {{question_1}} | What was your part? What happened? What would you change? |
| 2 | {{criterion_2}} | {{question_2}} | Who else was involved? How did you know it worked? |
| 3 | {{criterion_3}} | {{question_3}} | What was the hardest moment? |
| 4 | {{criterion_4}} | {{question_4}} | Can you give another example? |

Question rules:
- Ask about what they did ("tell me about a time"), not only what they would do.
- Probe for the facts: situation, their action, the result.
- Let there be silence. Do not fill it with hints.
- If they cannot answer, move on. Do not coach one candidate more than another.

## Never ask

Age, date of birth, gender, marital status, children or family plans, pregnancy, religion, nationality or origin, accent or language background (unless a language is a real job need and you ask about the skill), health or disability, photos, past pay, arrests (check local rules before any record question). If the candidate volunteers such a detail, do not note it and do not use it. <!-- bias-check: guidance -->

## Candidate questions (5 minutes)

Invite their questions. Answer honestly, including the hard parts of the job. Do not sell what is not true.

## Close (2 minutes)

Thank them. Repeat the next step and the date they will hear from you. Do not give a hint about the decision.

## Right after (10 minutes)

- Fill in the rating sheet within 15 minutes, while it is fresh. Score only what you heard, with a short quote or note for every score.
- Do not discuss with other interviewers before everyone has scored on their own. Then use `debrief.md`.
- Note anything that should change in the guide.

## Remote interviews

- Share the link and the plan in advance. Offer a phone option.
- Keep the same questions and the same time. Do not mark down a weak connection, a home background or a pet.
- Record only with the candidate's clear consent, and keep recordings for the shortest time you said you would. Not legal advice.

## Scoring reminders

- Score each criterion on its own, using the 0 to 4 levels in the scorecard.
- Level 3 needs a real example they did on their own with a clear result. Good intentions are level 1 at most.
- Write the evidence as a short quote or fact. If you cannot write one, the score is lower.
- A nervous start is not a weak answer. Give everyone the same warm up.
