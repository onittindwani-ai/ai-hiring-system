# Offer checklist: {{role_title}}

Use this before an offer goes out and until the person starts. Nothing is sent without the owner's yes. Not legal advice: ask a lawyer or employment adviser to check your contract and local rules.

Candidate: {{candidate_id}}   Owner approving: {{decider_name}}   Target start: {{start_date}}

## 1. Before you decide the offer

- [ ] The debrief is done and the decision and reason are written down in job related words.
- [ ] References are done for the finalist, with permission.
- [ ] You know the pay range and the real top of it ({{pay_range}}). The offer comes from the role's range and the scorecard, not from the person's past pay.
- [ ] You compared it with what people in the same role at your company earn. Fair pay inside the team matters.
- [ ] You know what you can add (start date, schedule, training budget, review date) in case the person asks.
- [ ] A backup candidate is ready in the pipeline, in case of a decline.

## 2. The offer

- [ ] Call the person first. Share the good news, the pay and the main terms.
- [ ] Send message 16 (`templates/messages/16-offer.md`) after the call, with the written terms attached or linked.
- [ ] Written terms include: title, pay and period, start date, hours and place, who they report to, trial or probation period if any, notice period, benefits, and how to accept.
- [ ] Give a fair time to decide (a few working days, not a few hours).
- [ ] If the person negotiates, answer honestly. Only change what you really can. Get the owner's yes before you change pay or terms.

## 3. After a yes

- [ ] Send message 19 (`templates/messages/19-welcome-accepted.md`).
- [ ] Get the signed contract. Check right to work and any checks the law allows, in the way local rules require.
- [ ] Move the pipeline stage to `accepted`.
- [ ] Tell the other finalists kindly (messages 14 and 15) once the offer is signed, not before.
- [ ] Set up accounts, equipment, access and a first week plan. Use `onboarding-30-60-90.md`.
- [ ] Move to `hired` on the first day.

## 4. After a no

- [ ] Thank them and ask (only if they want to share) what would have changed their mind.
- [ ] Move the pipeline stage to `declined`.
- [ ] Decide whether to go to the backup candidate. Same process, same fairness.
- [ ] Note what you learned (pay, timing, role scope) for the next hire.

## 5. Records

- [ ] Keep the decision record, scorecard and notes for the time your policy and local law say, then delete.
- [ ] Remove the job posts and the form, and mark the role filled on every place you posted it. Close the Google for Jobs page or markup.
- [ ] Tell everyone who is still in the process that the role is filled.

## Questions the candidate often asks (prepare honest answers)

- What does a normal week look like, and what is the hardest part of this job?
- How will my work be judged in the first 90 days, and who gives me feedback?
- What can I learn here, and is there a training budget?
- How are pay reviews decided, and when is the first one?
- How is the team doing? Why is this role open?

## Mistakes to avoid

- Using an exploding deadline ("answer in two hours") to pressure someone.
- Promising things you cannot give (a raise date, a title, a remote rule) just to close.
- Letting the other finalists hang for weeks. Tell them kindly as soon as the offer is signed.
- Skipping the written terms. A friendly call is not a contract.
- Cutting the offer after a first yes. If you must change the terms, explain why and apologize.
