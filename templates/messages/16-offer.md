# Offer

- **Use when:** The owner approved an offer for a finalist and the pay, start date and terms are decided and checked.
- **Who sends:** The owner, after calling the candidate first.
- **Channel:** Phone call first, then email (check local rules for written terms)
- **Length limit:** Under 180 words.

## Message

Subject: Offer for {{role_title}} at {{company_name}}

Hi {{candidate_first_name}},

It was great to talk with you, and we're happy to offer you the {{role_title}} role at {{company_name}}.

- Pay: {{pay_range}}, specifically {{offer_pay}}
- Start date: {{start_date}}
- Hours and place: {{hours_and_place}}
- Other terms: {{other_terms}}

This message summarizes our conversation. The full written terms are in {{contract_reference}}, and that document is what counts. Please take your time. We would like your answer by {{reply_by_date}}, and you can ask us anything before then.

We hope you join us.

{{recruiter_name}}

## Rules

- You (the owner) approve the exact text before it is sent. Claude only drafts it.
- Never invent a personal detail. Use `{{specific_detail}}` only for a real fact the person gave you in their application or in the conversation.
- Never promise a job unless this is the offer message and the offer is approved and real.
- No dashes, no hype, no pressure. Plain, warm and short.
- Call the person first. Never make an offer for the first time by email alone.
- The offer is only real if the owner approved the pay and terms and you have checked local rules on contracts. Not legal advice: ask a lawyer or employment adviser to review your contract template.
- Do not use pressure such as an exploding deadline. Give a fair time to decide.
- Do not base the pay on a candidate's pay history. Base it on the role's range and the scorecard.
